La Plata County short-term rental permits, step by step

By Casey Roberts · November 2026

There is no short-term rental permit in unincorporated La Plata County, as of November 2026. What you need is a Colorado sales tax license, your HOA's permission and a home that meets the county's good-neighbor guidelines. Inside Durango city limits it's different: you need a city permit first, and most are capped. Here are the steps for each.

Step 1: City or county?

Everything depends on this. Look up your parcel on the La Plata County assessor's map. A Durango mailing address doesn't mean the city: most of the Animas Valley, Hermosa and the Purgatory area are unincorporated county.

In the county: five steps

  1. Get your HOA's answer in writing. In the county, covenants are usually the real rule. Some subdivisions ban stays under 30 days; others set their own registration.
  2. Get a Colorado sales tax license. It's issued by the Colorado Department of Revenue, online. The state collects state sales tax, the county's 2% sales tax and the county lodging tax through it.
  3. Check what the platforms collect. Airbnb and Vrbo collect some taxes on their own bookings. You file and remit the rest, including every direct booking.
  4. Set the home up as the county recommends. A local contact who answers, shared with neighbors. Two guests per bedroom plus one. Parking on the property. Trash that keeps up. If you're on septic, ask La Plata County Public Health whether it can handle rental use.
  5. Insure it for rental. Most homeowner's policies exclude short-term rental use. Get a policy that covers it.

Hosting an event of 50 people or more, such as a wedding? That needs a separate county event permit.

Inside Durango city limits

  1. Check your zone. The city allows vacation rentals (stays of 1 to 29 nights) only in certain zones, and caps the permits in the main residential ones. Those caps are full, with waitlists.
  2. Apply for a Limited Use Permit before you advertise, and get a city business license.
  3. Register for city lodgers and sales taxes as well as the state license.
  4. Know the permit doesn't transfer. A sale ends it, and a buyer starts again.

Stays of 30 nights or more aren't regulated as vacation rentals by the city.

What could change

La Plata County lists short-term rentals among its planned code amendments, with a target of the third quarter of 2027. Nothing has been proposed in detail yet. If you're buying a home for its rental income, plan as if a county registration or permit could come, and watch for public hearings.

Questions owners ask

Do I need a short-term rental permit in La Plata County?

Not outside city limits, as of November 2026. Unincorporated La Plata County has no short-term rental permit or license. You do need a Colorado sales tax license to collect state and county sales tax and the county lodging tax, and your HOA has to allow it. The county lists short-term rental rules as a future code amendment, targeted for the third quarter of 2027.

Is my home in the city or the county?

Look up your parcel on the La Plata County assessor's map. An address that says Durango can still be in the county: most of the Animas Valley, Hermosa and Purgatory are.

Do Airbnb and Vrbo collect the taxes for me?

On their own bookings, they collect and remit some or all of the state and local taxes. Check each platform's tax page for your address, and remit anything they don't collect. Direct bookings are always yours, or your manager's, to collect.
