What a Durango vacation home earns, and what decides it

By Casey Roberts · October 2026

A typical Durango short-term rental earns somewhere between $41,000 and $55,000 a year, depending on whose data you trust. The top homes earn roughly double that, and almost everything that separates them is in the owner's control.

Below is what the three most-cited data firms report for Durango, what moves a home from average to excellent, and how to think about your own.

The market, three ways

Each firm counts listings and revenue differently, so their numbers don't match. Taken together, they draw a clear picture.

One trend shows up everywhere: nightly rates are rising (up 12–16% year over year) while occupancy is flat or falling. Guests are paying more, but they're choosier about where they stay. That rewards the homes that stand out.

Summer is the season. The shoulders decide the year.

July is Durango's best month, averaging about $7,260 per home; April is the slowest, at about $1,710. That's a fourfold swing. Summer, June through September, carries the year, with a second bump at Christmas and in March for spring skiing.

Almost every home fills in July. The difference between an average year and a great one is made in April, May, October and November, when guests need a reason to come.

What separates the top homes

BnbCalc puts Durango's high performers at around $77,000 a year and its low performers at around $19,000. The gap isn't luck. It comes down to five things:

1. Photography. Guests decide from the first image. Professional, naturally lit photos are the single cheapest upgrade an owner can make.

2. A hot tub. In Durango, homes with a hot tub are associated with about 22% more revenue (BnbCalc). After a day on the mountain, it's the amenity guests search for.

3. Pricing every day, not every season. With rates rising and occupancy falling, a set-and-forget price either leaves money on the table in July or sits empty in October. Rates should move with events, weather and demand.

4. Reviews and response time. Ranking on Airbnb and VRBO rewards five-star stays and fast replies. A local who answers in minutes, and fixes the hot tub the same afternoon, protects both.

5. A reason to come in the shoulder season. A fall aspen ride with bikes delivered, a spring ski weekend with lift tickets waiting. Homes that sell the trip, not just the beds, fill the months others don't.

What it means for your home

Start with your bedroom count and the table above, then ask honestly how your home scores on those five points. Every one of them is within an owner's control.
