Vacation rental management at Purgatory

By Casey Roberts · October 2026

Purgatory is the strongest rental market we serve. Most resort condos and homes can rent nightly, and about two-thirds of a home's year is earned from December through March. Here is what's allowed, what homes earn, and how we run them.

Can you rent here?

Purgatory sits in unincorporated La Plata County. The county has no short-term rental permit, so the real answer is your HOA. This is where things stand for the neighborhoods we work in:

NeighborhoodNightly rentals
Purgatory Village condos (Angelhaus, Eolus, Needles, Sitzmark, Twilight View, Silverpick)Yes
Durango Mountain townhomes (Tacoma Village, Travertine, Alpenglow)Yes
Lake Purgatory, Lake of the Pines and Beaver Circle homesYes, check covenants
Tamarron Lodge condosYes
Glacier Club homesNo
Cascade Village and homes north of the county lineSan Juan County rules

"Yes" means homes in that neighborhood rent nightly today. Covenants differ by building and can change, so we read yours before you sign. Checked October 2026.

Every stay owes the county's 2% lodging tax, 2% county sales tax and state sales tax. Airbnb and Vrbo collect some of these; we register for and remit the rest, including on direct bookings.

From our estimate model for Purgatory. "Owner keeps" is after booking fees, Ophir's 22% founding rate, utilities, supplies, snow removal, a repair reserve and rental insurance, before mortgage and property tax. Your home will differ; your own estimate takes 24 hours.

Winter carries the year. December through March brings in about two-thirds of the rent, July and August most of the rest. April, May and November are quiet, which is when we do the deep clean and the repairs, and when you can use the home yourself without costing much.

What guests come for

  • Ski days at Purgatory, with skis and boots sized and waiting in the gear room.
  • Lift-served mountain biking and the alpine coaster in summer.
  • Haviland Lake, Hermosa Creek Trail and the high passes toward Silverton.
  • A hot tub at the end of the day. In our model it's one of the biggest lifts to a mountain home's rent.

Homes we take here

Every Ophir home meets one standard. At Purgatory that also means a ski room or closet with a boot dryer, a plowed and reliable winter driveway, Wi-Fi of at least 100 Mbps, and beds for a group: most winter guests are families of six or more.

How we run a Purgatory home

  • The driveway is plowed and the walk cleared before every winter arrival.
  • We check heat, heat tape and pipes in every cold snap, occupied or not.
  • Rates move daily around holidays, spring break and powder forecasts.
  • Guests are met on arrival, and their trip is planned before they come.
  • Mud season brings a deep clean, a repair list for your approval and a photo report.

Questions owners ask

Can I rent my Purgatory condo or home nightly?

Usually yes. Unincorporated La Plata County, where Purgatory sits, has no short-term rental permit. The answer comes from your HOA covenants. Most resort condos and townhomes rent nightly today; Glacier Club homes do not. We check your documents before you sign anything.

What does a Purgatory vacation home earn?

In our model, a 2-bedroom condo earns about $40,000 a year in rent, a 3-bedroom townhome with a hot tub about $64,000, and a 4-bedroom home with a hot tub and views about $94,000. About two-thirds of it comes in December through March. We send a free estimate for your home within 24 hours.

I already have a manager. Can I switch in the middle of ski season?

Yes. We start the day after the last stay your current manager keeps, honor every reservation that moves to us, and handle the handoff. Many owners sign in the fall and start in January.
